Engagement is high. Sales aren’t. Here’s what I’d actually do.

“If social content gets engagement but sales conversion is low, what would you analyze first and what actions would you take?”

Here’s the honest truth: if people like the content but don’t buy, the content isn’t the problem — the path to the purchase is. So I wouldn’t touch the creative first. I’d follow the money backwards.

What I’d check first

  • Where people actually drop off. Post → link tap → product page → cart → checkout → paid. The leak is usually one or two steps, not the whole funnel. Find it before spending a baht.
  • Are the people liking it the people who’d buy? A wall of likes from non-buyers is the classic trap.
  • Can we even see a sale come from social? No coupon code, no tracking link — then “low conversion” might just mean we can’t measure it.
  • What the comments and DMs say. “ราคาเท่าไหร่”, “ซื้อที่ไหน” tells me instantly whether it’s price, trust, or just “I don’t know where to buy.”

What I’d do

  • Make the path stupidly short. One landing page — proof, offer, PromptPay, done. Every extra tap loses people.
  • Retarget the warm crowd. The people who already engaged get hit again with proof and a reason to buy now. That’s the fast win.
  • Micro-to-mid influencers with their own codes. Real people who actually train, not just pretty accounts. Funny or serious — but the product has to be the point. (And the code makes it measurable.)
  • Kill the price objection in the moment. A first-order trial or money-back beats discounting the brand.

How I’d know it worked

Social-attributed conversion rate up, cost per sale down — and for the first time, actually trackable.

Most “low conversion” problems aren’t a content problem. Good content gets attention. Conversion needs intent, a frictionless path, and a reason to buy today.
